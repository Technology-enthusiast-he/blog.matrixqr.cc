How to Create a vCard QR Code: Contact Sharing & Compatibility

By MatrixQR Studio • Published Aug 13, 2026 • Updated Oct 30, 2026 • 8 min read
DEFINITION: What is a vCard QR code?

A vCard QR code encodes structured contact details in a QR symbol. When someone scans it, a compatible camera or QR-scanning app may display a contact preview or offer an import action. The exact behavior depends on the device and app. MatrixQR currently creates vCard 3.0 payloads; an Apple Wallet pass is a separate, cryptographically signed format.

A digital business card QR code can make it easier to share your name, company, phone number, and email without retyping each detail. This guide shows how to create one with MatrixQR, explains the vCard 3.0 fields it generates, and covers phone-number formatting, privacy, and practical compatibility checks. It also explains why a vCard QR code is not the same thing as an Apple Wallet pass.

1. Format International Phone Numbers Clearly

If people in different countries will scan your contact QR code, include the country calling code rather than relying on a local dialing format. The ITU-T E.164 international numbering plan defines international public telephone numbers with a maximum of 15 digits, excluding the leading plus sign used in common written notation.

  • Use the international form: For example, write a US number as +14155550199 rather than a local-only form such as 415-555-0199.
  • Follow the country's numbering plan: Do not remove a leading national trunk prefix by applying a universal rule. Whether a zero is omitted depends on the country and the number type.
  • Do not assume formatting validates the number: MatrixQR encodes the phone value you enter; it does not verify that the number is assigned, reachable, or correctly formatted for every numbering plan.

2. Understand the vCard 3.0 Contact Fields

vCard is a standard for representing and exchanging contact information. RFC 2426 defines vCard 3.0, while RFC 6350 defines vCard 4.0 and obsoletes the earlier 3.0 specification. These versions are related but not interchangeable in every implementation.

MatrixQR currently generates vCard 3.0. The payload includes the required VERSION, N, and FN properties:

  • N: Structured name components in this order: family name, given name, additional names, honorific prefixes, and honorific suffixes.
  • FN: The formatted name shown to people, such as Alex Mercer.
  • ORG: The company or organization, when supplied.
  • TEL;TYPE=CELL: The mobile number, when supplied.
  • EMAIL: The email address, when supplied.

Here is an example of the vCard 3.0 structure produced when the corresponding MatrixQR fields are filled: 

BEGIN:VCARD
VERSION:3.0
N:Mercer;Alex;;;
FN:Alex Mercer
ORG:MatrixQR Studio
TEL;TYPE=CELL:+14155550199
EMAIL:alex@example.com
END:VCARD

The family and given name fields are optional in the MatrixQR form. If you leave them blank, the structured N property is still present, with empty components; the full name remains in FN. Text values also need correct escaping for characters such as backslashes, commas, semicolons, and embedded line breaks. Long content lines may need folding under the vCard rules.

3. Create and Export a vCard QR Code with MatrixQR

You can create a contact QR code directly in the MatrixQR Studio generator. The current vCard form supports a full name, family name, given name, company, mobile phone, and email address.

  1. Open MatrixQR Studio: Visit www.matrixqr.cc and select the vCard QR tab.
  2. Enter the formatted name: Fill in Full Name. This is used for the vCard FN property and is required to generate the contact QR code.
  3. Add structured name details: Optionally enter Family Name and Given Name. These fields populate the corresponding parts of the vCard N property. Enter them according to the contact's name structure; do not rely on automatic parsing of a full name.
  4. Add the contact details you want to share: Company, mobile phone, and email are optional. Use an international phone number when recipients may be outside your country.
  5. Generate the QR code: Select RUN MATRIX CORE. MatrixQR builds the vCard payload and renders the QR code in the browser.
  6. Export the image: Choose an available export format and select Export to save the QR image. The SVG option is an SVG wrapper containing an embedded PNG image, rather than a fully vectorized QR symbol.

4. Scan and Test Before Sharing

A QR code can be readable while a recipient's contact app still interprets one or more fields differently. Test the actual exported image on the devices and apps your audience is likely to use before printing it or adding it to a website.

  1. Test on more than one platform: Scan with an iPhone and an Android device, preferably using their built-in camera apps as well as the QR scanner your audience commonly uses.
  2. Review the contact preview: Confirm the formatted name, family and given names, company, phone number, and email appear in the intended fields.
  3. Check special characters: Test names or company names containing accented letters, commas, semicolons, apostrophes, or non-Latin characters if those occur in your real contact data.
  4. Check the phone number: Verify the country code and number are intact. A correctly encoded number does not guarantee that every device will dial or label it identically.
  5. Test the final artwork: Scan the downloaded image and, if it will be printed, scan a physical proof at the intended size and viewing distance.

A static vCard QR code contains the contact details encoded when it was created. If a phone number, email address, or company name changes later, generate a new code and replace old printed or published copies.

5. Protect Contact Details and Understand Apple Wallet

MatrixQR constructs the vCard payload and renders the QR code in your browser; the contact fields are not uploaded to a MatrixQR generation server for QR creation. Browser-side generation can reduce the amount of personal data sent to the generator, but it does not make the resulting QR code confidential or imply that a website makes no other network requests.

The QR image itself contains the contact data. Anyone who can photograph or scan a code displayed on a public sign, event badge, website, or social profile may be able to read the information. Share only details you intend to make available, and consider using a business phone number or role-based email address instead of a private home address or personal contact channel.

A vCard QR code is also different from an Apple Wallet pass. Apple Wallet passes are separate packages that include pass data and assets and must be cryptographically signed before distribution, as described in Apple's pass-building documentation. A vCard QR code does not automatically install or update a Wallet pass.

6. Frequently Asked Questions

Q: Does MatrixQR generate vCard 3.0 or vCard 4.0?

MatrixQR currently generates vCard 3.0. Its contact payload includes the required N and FN properties, plus the optional company, mobile phone, and email fields you enter.

Q: Which contact fields can I add?

The current form includes a required Full Name and optional Family Name, Given Name, Company, Mobile Phone, and Email Address fields. Family Name and Given Name populate the structured N property.

Q: Will scanning a vCard QR code automatically save a contact?

Not always. A compatible camera or QR scanner may show a contact preview or offer an import action, but the behavior depends on the device, operating-system version, and scanning app. Review the fields before saving.

Q: Is a vCard QR code private if it is generated in my browser?

Browser-side generation keeps the entered payload on your device during MatrixQR generation, but the contact details are still encoded in the QR image. Anyone who can scan or photograph a shared code may be able to read them.

Q: Can a vCard QR code be added to Apple Wallet as a pass?

No. A vCard QR code and an Apple Wallet pass are separate formats. Wallet passes require their own pass package and cryptographic signing process.

Conclusion

To create a useful vCard QR code, enter a clear formatted name, add structured name components and only the contact details you want to share, then test the exported code on the devices your recipients use. MatrixQR currently creates vCard 3.0 payloads in the browser. Correct formatting improves consistency, while real-device testing helps catch differences in scanning and contact-import behavior. Keep public contact information separate from details you want to keep private.
