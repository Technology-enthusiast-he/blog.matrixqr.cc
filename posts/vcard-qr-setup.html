vCard QR Codes and Apple Wallet Passes: Compatibility Guide

By MatrixQR Studio • Published Aug 13, 2026 • Updated Oct 21, 2026 • 7 min read
DEFINITION: What is a vCard QR code?

A vCard QR code stores contact information in a QR code so a compatible phone or contact app can read it. An Apple Wallet pass is a separate format: adding a pass generally requires a properly signed .pkpass package. A vCard QR code does not automatically become a Wallet pass, and neither format guarantees that saved details will remain current.

Exchanging contact details during international trade shows, cross-border corporate events, or global business conferences requires a solution that transcends paper cards. A standard vCard QR code can make contact details easier to share, while an Apple Wallet pass follows a different technical workflow. This guide explains phone-number formatting, common vCard fields, and key compatibility considerations; implementation requirements depend on the format and platform.

1. Standardize Country Codes for Global Compatibility

When sharing contact details across international borders, localized phone numbers (such as 010-1234-5678 or (02) 8765-4321) fail when saved by foreign attendees. According to the ITU-T E.164 International Telecommunication standard, phone numbers must follow strict global formatting:

  • Always prefix dialing codes with E.164 formatting: Structure numbers starting with a plus sign (+) followed by country code, area code, and subscriber number (e.g., +1-415-555-0199 for the US or +44-20-7123-4567 for the UK).
  • Strip localized trunk prefixes: Use the destination country's numbering plan to format the complete international number. Do not remove a leading zero by rule alone; trunk-prefix handling varies by country.

2. Use vCard 3.0 or 4.0 for Contact Data

For contact cards, IETF RFC 6350 specifies vCard 4.0. iOS Contacts can import supported vCard data, but field handling varies by OS version and app. Apple Wallet passes use a separate signed .pkpass format; a vCard payload alone is not a Wallet pass. Check the target platform's current requirements and test the fields you plan to share.

Ensure your payload incorporates essential standardized keys:

  • FN: Formatted Full Name.
  • ORG: Business Organization / Company Name.
  • TEL;TYPE=CELL: Mobile cell phone number formatted in E.164.
  • URL;TYPE=WORK: Direct link to portfolio or company domain.

3. Key Differences: Standard vCard QR Code vs. Apple Wallet Pass

A vCard QR code and an Apple Wallet pass serve different purposes, so they should not be treated as direct substitutes. A QR code can share contact details; a Wallet pass may support pass-specific features when configured and signed correctly. No comparative user-retention result is claimed here.

4. Step-by-Step: Sharing vCard Details and Adding a Separate Wallet Pass

If you need both formats, use these steps to prepare contact sharing and a separate Wallet pass:

  1. Create the Wallet pass separately: If you need an Apple Wallet pass, follow Apple's current pass format, signing, and distribution requirements. A vCard QR payload alone is not a signed .pkpass package.
  2. Optionally include a contact QR code: A pass design may include a QR code, but the encoded content and scanning behavior depend on how the pass is configured.
  3. Test Cross-Border Formatting: Test scanning across different region settings on iOS to verify that country dialing codes and postal codes render seamlessly.
  4. Provide a cross-platform option: Consider a standard vCard QR code or downloadable contact file for recipients who do not use Apple Wallet. Check the compatibility of the chosen format on target devices.
💡 Pro Tip: Privacy-First Digital Business Cards

When sharing contact details, check whether the generator processes the vCard in your browser or sends it to a remote service. Browser-side generation can reduce payload transfers to the generator, but it does not rule out page analytics, hosting logs, or processing by linked services.

5. Frequently Asked Questions (FAQ)

Q: How does an Apple Wallet pass differ from a vCard QR code?

A vCard QR code encodes contact details for a compatible scanner or contact app. An Apple Wallet pass is a separate signed .pkpass package; one does not automatically become the other.

Q: Why is E.164 format necessary for international vCard QR codes?

E.164 is an international numbering plan and commonly represents numbers with a leading plus sign and country code. Correct formatting improves consistency, but it cannot guarantee that every recipient's device or app will import or dial the number identically.

Q: Should I use vCard 3.0 or 4.0 for Apple iOS compatibility?

Apple platforms support common vCard formats, but field handling can vary by OS version, app, and vCard version. Test the fields you plan to share on the devices your recipients use.

Treat a vCard QR code and a signed Apple Wallet pass as separate formats, and test both on your target devices.
